# How to Play — Game Instructions

## Introduction

**Vaporize** is a deck-building card game for 2–4 players. The player leads one of five classes. Every class has its own character board, its own resource and its own cards, but all of them play by the same basic rules in this book.

You begin with a small deck of 12 cards. On your turn you play cards from your hand for free: influence cards give you influence to spend, spells take effect at once, and units enter your board. With influence you buy stronger cards from the markets, remove weak cards from your deck, and develop your units up through four tiers. The higher a unit stands, the more victory points it scores at the end of each of your turns.

There is no player health. Units fight only other units, and victory points are the only way to win.

## Object of the game

Be the first to reach **30 victory points (VP)**. Once someone gets there, the round is played to the end so everyone has the same number of turns, and the player with the most VP wins.

You score VP mostly with units standing on high tiers, and with cards that grant VP.

## Your character board

Your character board holds your class's rules and all of your trackers. Every class's board has the same parts. The Mage board is shown here.


1.  **Class name and primary elements.** The two elements that most of your class's cards carry.

2.  **Passives.** Rules that are always active for your class. A *Start of turn* or *End of turn* label shows when a passive takes effect.

3.  **Prepare.** What your class does whenever a card tells you to prepare.

4.  **Invoke.** How your class produces a value whenever a card tells you to invoke.

5.  **Class resource.** The track for your class's own resource (mana for the Mage), with any limit marked on it.

6.  **Units.** Your unit tiers: how many spots each tier has, what it costs to develop a unit into it, and how many VP each unit there scores at the end of your turn. Tier 4 is shared by all players.

7.  **Victory points.** One marker on the tens row and one on the ones row. The gold box marks the goal of 30.

8.  **Card removal cost.** What it costs to remove a card from your deck. Start on the first space. Each time you remove a card, pay the cost shown, then move the marker 1 space.

Lay your unit cards in rows beside your board, one row for each tier.

## The cards

There are three types of cards, and playing any of them is free.

  - **Influence** cards give you influence (inf), the currency of the game. Many also have a small extra effect.

  - **Spells** have a one-time effect when you play them.

  - **Units** go onto your board and stay there until they are destroyed. The number in the bottom-left corner is the unit's attack, and the bottom-right one is its health.

**Buy cost.** The number in the top-right corner is the card's price in a market. Cards in your starting deck show 0.

**Elements.** Most cards carry one of five elements, shown in the top-left corner: fire, water, wind, lightning or shadow. Some carry two, a few carry all five (**\[all\]**), and a few carry none. Many cards get stronger when you have played enough cards of one element earlier in the same turn, or when your discard pile holds enough of them.

## Setup

1.  **Choose a class.** Each player takes a character board, the 12-card starting deck of that class (cards marked *Starter*) and its 15 private-market cards (marked *Private*). Return the cards of unused classes to the box.

2.  **Set your tracks.** Put a marker on 0 on both victory point rows, on the first space of the card removal cost track, and on 0 on your class resource track. Then do any extra setup printed on your character board.

3.  **Draw your hand.** Shuffle your starting deck face down and draw 7 cards.

4.  **Build the shared market.** Shuffle all shared-market cards into one face-down deck and place it on the left. Reveal 6 cards in a row to its right.

5.  **Build your private market.** Shuffle your private-market cards face down beside your board and reveal 3 of them.

6.  **Make room for Tier 4** in the middle of the table. It has 1 spot for each player, minus 1: 1 spot with 2 players, 2 with 3 players, 3 with 4 players.

7.  **Put all tokens** within everyone's reach.

8.  **Choose the first player** at random and give them the first player marker. The same player starts every round, so nobody ever takes two turns in a row.

## Playing a turn

Starting with the first player, each player takes a turn in clockwise order. When everyone has had a turn, the round is over and a new one begins with the same first player.

Your turn has three phases, always in this order:

1.  **Start of turn**

2.  **Actions**

3.  **End of turn**

> **Golden rule:** when a card or your character board contradicts this rulebook, follow the card or the board.

### 1\. Start of turn

Resolve all **Start of turn (SOT)** effects on your units and your character board, in any order you choose.

### 2\. Actions

Take any of these actions, in any order, as many times as you can pay for them:

  - **Play a card** (free). An influence card gives you the influence shown and resolves any other text on it. A spell resolves its text. A unit goes into a free spot on your Tier 1 (see *Units, tiers and combat*). Cards you play stay face up in front of you until the end of your turn.

  - **Buy a card** (its price in inf). Take a card from the shared market or from your own private market and put it on your discard pile (see *The shared market*).

  - **Refresh a market** (your first refresh of the shared market each turn is free; every other refresh costs 1 inf). See *The shared market*.

  - **Remove a card** (the cost on your removal track). Remove 1 card in your hand or your discard pile from the game, then move your marker 1 space. Every cost appears twice on the track, so the price goes up after every second removal.

  - **Develop a unit** (2 tier -\> 3inf, 3 tier -\> 4inf and 4 tier -\> 5 inf). Move one of your units up one tier.

  - **Attack with a unit** (free). Each of your units can attack once per turn.

These rules apply during the whole turn:

  - **Keep at least 2 cards in your hand.** You can never play or remove your last 2 cards. With 7 cards in hand, you can use at most 5 of them.

  - **Influence doesn't carry over.** Spend it this turn or lose it.

  - **Empty deck.** Whenever you need to draw, scout or otherwise use cards from your deck and it is empty, shuffle your discard pile to form a new deck, then carry on. If your deck holds fewer cards than an effect asks for, the effect uses only the cards that are left: with 2 cards in your deck, Scout 4 looks at just those 2.

  - **Effects at the same time: you choose the order.** When two or more effects would happen at the same moment, you decide in which order they resolve. *Example: the Robot starter unit has health equal to invoke + 1 and the text "When played: gain 1 heat". Both happen as it enters play, so its owner may gain the heat first and then invoke, or invoke first.*

  - **The order of your actions matters.** Each card and action resolves completely before the next one, and it only sees what has already happened this turn. Cards you play later never change a card you've already played.

*Example: the Mage starter spell (fire) reads "Deal invoke damage. If you played at least 2 water cards this turn, gain Split." If you play two water influence cards first and then the spell, it gains Split. If you play the spell first, it doesn't, even if you play the water cards afterwards.*

### 3\. End of turn

Do these steps in any order:

1.  **Score.** Resolve all **End of turn (EOT)** effects on your units and your character board. Then score VP for your units on the tiers: 1 VP for each unit on Tier 2, 2 VP on Tier 3 and 3 VP on Tier 4.

2.  **Untap** all your units, and remove one token of each status effect, such as stun or freeze, from each of them (see *Status effects*).

3.  **Clean up.** Put all the cards you played this turn on your discard pile. Unspent influence is lost. Cards still in your hand stay there.

4.  **Draw** back up to 7 cards, one at a time. If your deck runs out partway through, shuffle your discard pile into a new deck and keep drawing.

Then the next player starts their turn.

## The shared market

The shared market is a row of 6 face-up cards next to the shared market deck. New cards enter on the left, slide to the right as the game goes on, and get cheaper on the way.


the shared market · 6 spots

  - **Prices.** The leftmost card costs 1 inf more than printed, and the rightmost card costs 1 inf less. The 4 cards in between cost their printed price.

  - **Buying.** Pay the card's price and put it on your discard pile. Reveal a new card from the deck into the empty spot.

  - **Refreshing.** On your turn you may refresh the shared market once for free. Every further refresh of the shared market, and every refresh of your private market, costs 1 inf. To refresh, remove the rightmost card from the game, slide all the other cards one spot to the right, and reveal a new card in the leftmost spot.

  - **Only the rightmost card can leave a market this way.** Cards with the *Refresh* keyword work the same way.

  - **Removed cards.** Cards removed from a market are set aside face up, and anyone may look through them at any time. When a market's deck runs out, shuffle the cards removed from that market to form its new deck. If there are none, the empty spots stay empty.

**Private markets.** Your class's private market shows 3 cards in a row. Only you can buy from it or refresh it, and its cards always cost their printed price. When you buy one, reveal a new card in its place.

## Units, tiers and combat

Units are how you score every turn: the higher a unit stands, the more VP it earns and the more inf it took to get it there. Combat is how you knock rival units off the board.

### Tiers

Each player has three tiers of their own, Tiers 1–3. Tier 4 is shared by all players. Every tier has a limited number of spots, printed on your character board, and a unit needs a free spot to enter it.

| Tier            | Spots         | Develop into it      | VP per unit at the end of your turn |
| --------------- | ------------- | -------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Tier 1          | on your board | — (units enter here) | 0                                   |
| Tier 2          | on your board | 3 inf                | 1                                   |
| Tier 3          | on your board | 4 inf                | 2                                   |
| Tier 4 (shared) | players − 1   | 5 inf                | 3                                   |

  - **Playing a unit.** It goes into a free spot on your Tier 1, and you place HP tokens on it equal to its health. If one of its stats shows the invoke symbol, invoke once as it enters; that value then stays fixed. If your Tier 1 is full, you can't play a unit.

  - **Developing.** Pay the cost to move one of your units up one tier into a free spot. You can't skip a tier, and developing doesn't heal or untap a unit.

  - **Tier 4** is first come, first served. While its spots are full, nobody can develop into it.

  - **Moving down.** Units only move down when a card degrades them.

  - **Summoned units** are tokens. They count as units on your Tier 1 but don't take up a spot there.

### Attacking

1.  **Choose an attacker:** any of your untapped units, including one you played this turn. A unit with 0 attack can't attack.

2.  **Choose a target:** one enemy unit on the same tier as the attacker or one tier higher. Never a lower tier.

3.  **Deal damage** equal to the attacker's attack by removing that many HP tokens from the target. The target doesn't strike back.

4.  **Tap the attacker** (turn it sideways). It untaps at the end of your turn.

You can't attack players or your own units, and each attack goes to a single target.


attack range · own tier and one tier higher

A Tier 1 unit can never reach Tier 3. Moving a unit up takes it out of reach of the lowest units, but it can no longer hit them either.

### Damage and destruction

  - **Damage stays.** HP tokens aren't restored between turns. Only effects that give HP heal a unit.

  - **Destroyed units.** When a unit's health reaches 0, it is destroyed and goes to its owner's discard pile. Its spot is free at once. Summoned tokens are removed from the game instead.

  - **Damage from spells and abilities** follows the card's own targeting, such as "a unit on tiers 1–3". The attack range applies only to attacks.

Some keywords bend these rules: *Shield*, *Stun*, *Freeze*, *Degrade* and *Split*. See *Keywords*.

### Status effects

Stun, Shield and Freeze are status effects. Each puts a token on the unit, and tokens of the same kind stack.

  - A unit stays affected as long as it has at least one token of that kind.

  - At the end of its owner's turn, remove only **one** stun or freeze token from it.

  - When shielded unit takes any instance of damage, only one shield is removed at a time.

*Example: a unit with 3 stun tokens can't attack during its owner's next 3 turns.*

## End of the game

When any player reaches **30 VP**, finish the current round: every player who hasn't had a turn this round takes theirs. Then the game ends. VP gained or lost during those last turns still counts, so a player who reached 30 first can still be caught.

The player with the most VP wins. If 2 or more players share the same VP, the one with a unit on the highest tier wins. If there is still a tie, the game ends in a draw between those players.

## Example of a round

### Other terms and explanations

  - "Other" cards played this turn**.** "If you played at least 2 other water cards this turn" counts only the cards you played *before* this card. It never counts the card itself, and cards you play later in the same turn don't count either. The order in which you play your cards matters.

  - "In your discard pile" counts the cards there right now, including the cards played in given turn. Your discard pile is face up, and anyone may look through it.

  - Ally and enemy units. Ally units are yours; enemy units belong to other players.

  - Tier ranges, such as "a unit on tiers 1–3" or "a unit on a tier where you have a unit", limit what a card can target. All effects can target any unit (both ally and enemy) unless specified

  - If any card has a negative effect written on it as a part of the text, it's required to play the card ex. the card with text "Deal 1 dmg to any of your units and gain 3 inf" requires you to have a unit in play that can receive this damage. If you cannot meet this requirement, or do not want to, the card cannot be played.

  - You can, however, omit any positive or neutral effects on the cards. You can the same way "play" a card without any of it's positive effects if you need to get rid of it for some reason

## Frequently asked questions

### Units and combat

  - **Can a unit attack on the turn it is played?** Yes, once, like any other unit.

  - **Can a unit develop after attacking?** Yes. It stays tapped and can't attack again this turn.

  - **Can a unit attack after developing?** Yes, if it hasn't attacked yet this turn. It uses the range of its new tier.

  - **Can a frozen unit attack?** Yes. Freeze only stops it from developing.

  - **When does a unit with an invoke stat invoke?** Once, as it enters play. The value stays fixed afterwards.

  - **Is there a maximum health?** No. Effects that give HP add tokens, even above the printed health.

  - **Does a shield stop Split damage?** It absorbs only the damage assigned to the shielded unit. Other units still take theirs.

  - **Can I use a spot freed this turn?** Yes. A spot frees the moment a unit is destroyed or moves, so you can play or develop into it straight away.

  - **Can I summon a token when my Tier 1 is full?** Yes. Tokens don't take up a spot.

### Cards, hand and markets

  - **Does removing a card from my hand count toward the 2-card minimum?** Yes. Your last 2 cards can't leave your hand by your own actions.

  - **Do multi-element cards count once or for each element?** For each element. A fire-and-water card counts as a fire card and as a water card.

  - **Can I use a card I just bought?** Not this turn. It goes to your discard pile and reaches your hand after a later reshuffle.

  - **Can I buy from another class's private market?** No. Only from the shared market and your own private market.

  - **Can I remove a card I played this turn?** Yes. The removed card needs to be either the one from the hand or the discard pile, and cards played in a given turn go to the discard pile. Every interaction with the discard pile should include cards played this turn as well.
